What CleanBid is

CleanBid is a software tool. You give it facts about a building and your business — square footage, tasks, crew wages, overhead, the margin you want — and it applies standard arithmetic to produce an estimate, a price, and a proposal document you can print or share. That is all it does. It is a calculator and a document builder, running on assumptions that you control.

Not professional advice

CleanBid is none of the following:

  • an accountant or bookkeeping system;
  • a tax adviser — it does not calculate sales tax, VAT/GST, or payroll taxes and does not advise on what's taxable;
  • a lawyer — proposal terms text in CleanBid is boilerplate you can edit, not legal advice;
  • a financial adviser — margin targets are numbers you choose, not recommendations;
  • an insurance adviser — bonding or insurance figures only appear if you type them in;
  • an employment-law adviser — wage and burden fields are whatever you enter, and compliance with wage laws is up to you;
  • a guaranteed pricing authority — nothing here certifies a price is right for your market.

For accounting, tax, legal, insurance, or employment questions, talk to a qualified professional who knows your business.

Estimates, not guarantees

Every number CleanBid produces is an estimate derived from the inputs and assumptions configured at the time. Real jobs can run long, crews call out, buildings differ from their survey, and costs move. A CleanBid quote reflects your assumptions — not a promise about what a job will actually cost or earn. Whether and how you commit to a price is always your decision.

What you remain responsible for reviewing

Before any estimate leaves your desk, check the things the app cannot know:

  • labor assumptions — wage rates, burden, productivity per task, crew hours;
  • material and supply costs;
  • overhead recovery;
  • margins and discounts;
  • taxes and fees (which CleanBid does not calculate);
  • service scope and frequency;
  • measurements and site conditions from your survey;
  • customer names, addresses, and contact details;
  • the final quoted price;
  • contractual terms you include with the proposal.
